# Skill-assessment appeals and independent adjudication

## Purpose and status

T014 / [#357](https://github.com/solana-hackathon-1/useRAID.fun/issues/357), blueprint 09.7 (with 08.6, 09.4, 17.4), under R15/R16, I07/I10 and [DEC-2026-11-13-12](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0222-owner-delegated-decisions--13-november-2026) as confirmed by DEC-2026-11-14-11. A participant may appeal the current decided version of their own skill assessment once; an independent adjudicator decides it once. **Default-off** behind `SKILL_ASSESSMENT_APPEALS_ENABLED`. Status: implemented and tested against real PostgreSQL, with the #490 follow-ups (related-Party conflict, the exclusion of original reviewers from the fresh review, the overdue operator alert, no id-existence oracle); UAT, starting the fresh review, the adjudicator case view and the owner's confirmation of the provisional windows are **NOT VERIFIED / not delivered** (see Boundaries).

An appeal moves no money and creates no permission, capacity or rank. It never edits `SkillAssessment` or `SkillAssessmentVersion`: the appealed decision stays in effect while pending, after a denial, and after an upheld appeal until a fresh review separately issues a new version. Nothing here grants a rank automatically.

## Interface

`src/skill-appeals/` (storage in `storage.ts`, contracts in `contracts.ts`, pure rules in `policy.ts`, transport in `http.ts`):

| Function                                                                                      | Authority                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `fileSkillAssessmentAppeal(tx, {actor, input, idempotencyKey, requestId}, options)`           | the signed-in HUMAN participant who owns the assessment; the named reference must be the assessment's current version (`REVISION_CHANGED`)                                                                                                        |
| `adjudicateSkillAssessmentAppeal(tx, {actor, input:{appealId, outcome, reason}, …}, options)` | a live session on the host allowlist who passes the independence rules; `UPHELD` or `DENIED`, once                                                                                                                                                |
| `listOverdueSkillAssessmentAppeals(tx, {limit})`                                              | trusted operator-side caller (an alert pass); ids and seconds only                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `createOverdueAppealAlerter({list, log, now})` (`overdue-alert.ts`)                           | the worker step `skill-appeal-overdue`: one value-free line `{alert:'skill-appeal-overdue', count, atLeast, oldestOverdueSeconds, appealIds}` (at most 10 ids), repeated no sooner than every 15 minutes per process; reads only, decides nothing |

`POST /api/v1/skill-assessment-appeals` files; `POST /api/v1/skill-assessment-appeals/{appealId}/decision` decides. Both need the session cookie, same-origin, an `Idempotency-Key` and a bounded strict JSON body; the appeal id comes only from the path. Refusals for self, original-reviewer, Guild-relationship, Party-relationship and not-listed are one opaque `FORBIDDEN` (the pure policy distinguishes them; storage and transport do not), and so is an appeal id that does not exist: an allowlisted account cannot tell a missing appeal from one it is barred from. A Party relationship is the adjudicator and the appellant both being on one **formed** Party roster: a proposal in which every named member recorded consent, counting the Party leader and those members (agent default under DEC-2026-11-13-12 for 17.4 "related Party"; owner may revisit). An unconsented, declined or abandoned proposal never counts, so being named in a proposal cannot force an adjudicator to recuse.

Results never carry the statement, an account id, the evaluators, the adjudicator or the excluded list, and are never an authorization token (`changesAssessment`, `grantsRank`, `isAuthorizationToken` are literal `false`).

## Locks, replay and flags

Order, matching decisions and their triggers (case before person/skill): the receipt, then User/Session (and the HUMAN participant for filing); for **adjudication** the case locks (class 12020, one per submission of the assessment, sorted) so an in-flight review, ownership or decision commits before the original reviewers are read; then the per-person/skill assessment lock (class 12022, shared with decisions and acceptance); then the appeal row (`FOR UPDATE`). Filing takes 12022 only. If a version for a further submission appears while waiting, the whole transaction retries (`RETRY_TRANSACTION`) rather than lock out of order. Authority and the allowlist are re-proven after every wait. The independence check runs before the appeal's state is revealed, so a conflicted adjudicator gets the same single opaque `FORBIDDEN` whether the appeal is pending or already decided. The flag gates only NEW filings and decisions; an original receipt still replays. Concurrent adjudicators serialize and exactly one decides; concurrent filings serialize and exactly one succeeds (`APPEAL_EXISTS` for the rest). A filing that races a new assessment decision either commits first (the appeal then names the version that was current when filed) or fails `REVISION_CHANGED`. Residual window: a review assignment issued but not yet used by any recorded review is not an original reviewer, and assignments take no 12020 lock; filing and deciding use separate throttle buckets.

## Host dependencies and consuming example

A host supplies: the transaction port (`READ_COMMITTED` PostgreSQL), actor session facts, the allowlist predicate `isAdjudicator(accountId)`, the two window values, and the write switch. [Independent adjudicator host](../examples/skill-assessment-appeals/README.md) consumes the pure independence and window rules with its own synthetic facts and no application, database or environment dependency. The Root host is `src/lib/skill-appeals-host.ts` and `skill-appeals-http.ts`.

## Boundaries (not built, not decided here)

- **Reopening the assessment.** Delivered: an upheld appeal records the requirement and the excluded reviewers, and the database refuses an excluded original reviewer as the evaluator of a new review appointment, review version or assessment decision on that assessment (a brand-new submission with no assessment version yet is not covered). **Not built, owner-open:** starting the fresh review itself (who appoints the fresh reviewer, the review path for an already decided version, the state of the resulting correcting assessment version and its 09.4 event); no rank is granted before it.
- **Adjudicator case view.** What an adjudicator may read of the statement, evidence and original review is an access decision (identity/access grants) that no decision has made; there is still no read route and nothing new is disclosed.
- **Operator alert.** Delivered as the `skill-appeal-overdue` worker step (log line only; no pager, no per-appeal acknowledgement state, no automatic outcome).
- **Conflicts.** Guild and Party relationships are checked. **Financial interest (17.4) is not observable** (no recorded fact for an adjudicator's financial interest in a skill assessment) and token-holder membership is not consulted (an OPEN holder-observation matter). The Party relationship is checked under the locks but no Party lock exists, so a Party formed in the same instant as a decision is not serialized against it.
- **No participant UI** and no notification of the outcome.
- The 14-day windows are provisional and the owner may revisit them; changing them never rewrites filed appeals (the deadline is stored per row).
