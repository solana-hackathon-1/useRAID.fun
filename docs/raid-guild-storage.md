# Persisted Guilds, Founder authority and scoped grants

## Purpose and status

T009 storage core under R01–R04/R10/R15/R16, I06/I07/I10/I13, AC07/AC73–75 and blueprint 06.1–06.4, 25.2, 28.1. It persists a Guild from an owner's private [draft](raid-guild-drafts.md), keeps immutable profile history, defines scoped role grants and exposes the Guild's current authority. It builds on the pure [profile policy](raid-guild-profiles.md) (`evaluateGuildProfilePermission`), which it calls with facts rebuilt inside the command transaction. It adds no HTTP route, UI, feature flag, token recognition, money, capacity or work effect. Project-control verification (T010 evidence review, O11/G10), ownership transfer, public updates, the directory (T015) and the wiring of the work boundary's `authorize` to these records are separate slices and remain NOT VERIFIED. Independent review, UAT and business sign-off remain NOT VERIFIED.

## Authority model

- **Founder** is the account that owned the draft at activation. It changes only through the consented ownership-transfer process below (a database guard rejects every other write) and implies **profile** and **work** management only.
- **Activation** (`activateGuild`) is performed by an approver account the host authorizes (`GuildActivationHost.authorizeActivation`). The module stores the approver and an opaque `activationRef` naming the host's evidence record. The module does not decide project control: the module itself refuses an approver who is the draft owner (whatever the host policy answers), so Founder control is always approved by someone other than the Founder, and a token balance, market metadata or X/admin allowlist never creates a Guild or Founder.
- **Grants** (`GuildRoleGrant`) have a `kind`: `PROFILE` (names the editable fields, `name` and/or `description`), `WORK`, `TREASURY` or `DISPUTE`. Only `PROFILE` grants carry fields; the others are whole-scope and cannot delegate. A grant is live only inside its `[validFrom, expiresAt)` window, evaluated on the database clock, and until revoked. Kinds never imply one another, and the Founder holds no treasury or dispute authority by role (blueprint 06.2). Grants are issued and revoked only through `grantGuildRole` / `revokeGuildRole` (below); the table guard allows only a one-time revocation and never an edit or delete. Rows inserted directly by a privileged writer (as tests do to place a window in the past) carry no receipt.
- **Holder eligibility** is never an input. Token holding grants no administration, treasury, dispute or code permission (AC07).
- **Cross-Guild writes** are denied: authority is looked up per Guild id, so a Founder or grantee of one Guild has nothing in another.

Grant rules (DEFAULT, not an owner decision): nobody grants themselves a role and the Founder is never a grantee (they already hold profile and work by role, and treasury/dispute authority is issued to other accounts only); only `PROFILE` grants carry fields and may delegate; a delegate passes on only fields they hold with delegation rights, without delegation of their own, with an explicit expiry no later than their own cover (implemented, [DEC-2026-11-14-01](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0223-owner-delegated-defaults--14-november-2026): every delegate-issued grant needs an explicit `expiresAt`, also no later than 90 days after issuance on the database clock, `DELEGATED_GRANT_MAX_DAYS`, provisional, and an application rule only (a direct database writer is not bound by it, like the other delegation rules; the cap is exactly 90 x 24 hours from the locked database instant); a missing, `null` or later-than-90-days expiry is `INVALID_INPUT`, HTTP 422 `INVALID_COMMAND`, judged after the delegate's standing and cover so it tells a non-delegate nothing; an expiry past the delegate's own bounded cover stays `FORBIDDEN`; Founder-issued grants are unchanged, expiry optional and uncapped; renewal is a new grant and nothing is extended in place; existing unbounded passed-on grants are not revoked or edited, they stay in force until revoked, cascaded or the delegate's cover ends. Before rollout count existing unbounded passed-on grants (`grantedByAccountId` not the Founder, `expiresAt IS NULL`, unrevoked) and report them to each Guild's Founder), at most 10 live grants each; at most 200 live grants per Guild (`GRANT_LIMIT`); a past expiry is `INVALID_INPUT`; a suspended grantee is `ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED`. Grant and revoke judge standing first without a lock (a stranger gets `FORBIDDEN` for a missing Guild, a missing grant and an existing one alike, and cannot stall the Guild), recheck it under the lock, and so serialize with profile edits: an edit sees a committed revocation or runs before it. Revoking a delegating grant also revokes each still-live grant that grantee passed on unless, for every field of it, they still hold another live delegating grant covering that field and lasting at least as long (expired rows grant nothing and are left as history). Role separation ([DEC-2026-11-13-04](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0222-owner-delegated-decisions--13-november-2026) a–d, migration `202611130007_guild_role_separation`; example: `role-separation.sql`): while funds are simulated the Founder may issue `TREASURY` and `DISPUTE` grants without a second approver. The database (not only `grantGuildRole`) refuses a live `TREASURY`/`DISPUTE` grant naming the Guild's current Founder, on grant insert and on a Founder change, and refuses a live grant of one of the two kinds to an account that holds a live grant of the other in the same Guild (live = unrevoked and unexpired at the database clock; a not-yet-started grant counts; the same kind twice, other accounts and other Guilds are unaffected). Both triggers take the Guild row lock first (`FOR UPDATE`), so a direct writer serializes with every Guild command, with a concurrent grant and with a Founder change, and the later one sees the earlier one's committed result. `grantGuildRole` refuses the same cases early with `FORBIDDEN`, and also refuses a `TREASURY`/`DISPUTE` grant to the recipient of an open (`PROPOSED`, unexpired) ownership proposal (`FORBIDDEN`) instead of only at acceptance; other kinds are unaffected. The migration checks existing data first and fails loudly, naming the number and first ten grant ids, if any live grant already violates the invariant; it never revokes or edits a row. An operator revokes the offending grant through the application (or corrects the Founder), runs `prisma migrate resolve --rolled-back 202611130007_guild_role_separation` and re-runs `migrate deploy`. The script has no explicit `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` so it runs as one implicit transaction and the runner reports the original error. Migration `202611130008_guild_role_separation_recheck` (example: `role-separation-recheck.sql`; #471 item 1) repeats that report under `LOCK TABLE "GuildRoleGrant","Guild" IN SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE MODE` (with a 30-second `lock_timeout`), because 007's report ran without a lock and a violating grant could commit before its `CREATE TRIGGER` and escape both; it changes nothing and fails with `resolve them first` the same way (recovery: revoke or correct, `prisma migrate resolve --rolled-back 202611130008_guild_role_separation_recheck`, re-run `migrate deploy`). Assumptions to keep: a direct writer locks the Guild row first (`FOR UPDATE`) before writing a grant or the Founder, since a writer holding only `FOR KEY SHARE` that then reaches the trigger's `FOR UPDATE` can deadlock (Postgres aborts one with `40P01`; retry); the triggers rely on READ COMMITTED (a fresh snapshot after the Guild lock), so a direct writer at REPEATABLE READ or SERIALIZABLE must not rely on them alone; and DEC (d), the early refusal for the recipient of an open proposal, is enforced only in `grantGuildRole`, not by the database, whose Founder-change trigger still refuses the acceptance if a direct writer grants it anyway. Tests (`tests/guild-role-separation-db.test.ts`, real PostgreSQL, separate transactions) cover a Founder change that commits while a grant to the new Founder waits (refused), a grant that commits while the Founder change waits (change refused), a multi-row `INSERT` and a data-modifying CTE giving one account both kinds (refused), and the locked recheck waiting for an in-flight writer. Deferred, not implemented (item e, provisional, owner may revisit): a second approval by a separate operator for issuing `TREASURY`/`DISPUTE` before any real-funds flag is enabled for a Guild; its process is defined with O05/O06.

Ownership change (DEFAULT process, not an owner decision): the Founder proposes, the recipient accepts with their own session, and the host's `authorizeOwnershipTransfer(tx, {guildId, fromAccountId, toAccountId})` must approve (the hook for a recorded re-verification of project control, O11/G10). The Founder changes only through that accepted transfer: the `Guild` guard rejects any other Founder write, an accepted transfer must be applied before the transaction ends, and a decided transfer is final. A direct writer cannot decide on a party's behalf: CHECKs bind the decider (the recipient for accept/decline, the proposer for withdraw) and the decision time to the proposal window, and a deferred trigger requires, at commit, the proposing Founder's `ProposeOwnershipTransfer` receipt and the deciding account's `RespondOwnershipTransfer` receipt (or, for a lapse recorded by a later proposal, that Founder's `ProposeOwnershipTransfer` receipt). A privileged writer who can also fabricate command receipts is outside this guard, as for the other Guild tables. A response to a lapsed proposal (ACCEPT, DECLINE or WITHDRAW alike) records it as `EXPIRED` instead of applying it and returns `status: 'EXECUTED'` with `transfer.state: 'EXPIRED'`, `applied: false` and audit `guild.ownership_expired.v1`; this is not an error, so clients must check `transfer.state` (or `applied`), never only `status`, before treating ownership as moved. The decision is stamped once, so an acceptance whose host hook runs past `expiresAt` is recorded the same way. `authorizeOwnershipTransfer` runs while the Guild and transfer rows are locked: it must be fast and use only the given transaction (no network calls), or it blocks every write to that Guild and holds a connection. Treasury and dispute authority stay separate from the Founder role (blueprint 06.2): as `grantGuildRole` never makes the Founder a grantee, a proposal to an account holding an unrevoked, unexpired (including not yet started) `TREASURY` or `DISPUTE` grant in the Guild is `FORBIDDEN`, and acceptance re-checks it (a grant issued after the proposal also refuses with `FORBIDDEN`, leaving the proposal open; the recipient may surrender that grant and accept again). `PROFILE` and `WORK` grants held by the recipient are not revoked by a transfer. The former Founder keeps no Founder role and loses revoke power over grants they issued while Founder, `TREASURY`/`DISPUTE` included ([DEC-2026-11-13-03](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0222-owner-delegated-decisions--13-november-2026) a): `revokeGuildRole` admits the issuer only while the issuer still holds the authority it issued under, and those grants stay in force until the new Founder or the grantee revokes them; a former Founder who later receives a live delegating `PROFILE` grant can revoke only `PROFILE` grants whose fields it covers (a literal reading: the rule does not record under which authority a grant was issued); the profile revision and history do not change. Because the cascade keys on the issuing account (unchanged), a new Founder who earlier held a delegating profile grant still loses, when that grant is revoked, the `PROFILE` grants they issued (also those issued later as Founder) that no other delegating grant of theirs covers. Ids in commands are canonicalized to lowercase, so an uppercase UUID is the same request as its lowercase form. Responses and replays are for the two parties, judged by identity. Standing is judged before the Guild row is locked (#355): `proposeOwnershipTransfer` needs the current Founder, and `respondOwnershipTransfer` needs the right party of the named transfer (the recipient to accept or decline, the proposing Founder to withdraw) or, for an unknown transfer id, the Founder; any other account gets `FORBIDDEN` for a missing Guild, a missing transfer and a real one alike and cannot stall the Guild, and the same rule is judged again under the lock. A recipient who names a transfer that is not theirs is such a stranger (`FORBIDDEN`, not `NOT_FOUND`). The host hook returns only a boolean and the module persists nothing about the verified relationship beyond the accepted transfer and its audit event: the host owns any re-verification record, and rows it writes in the given transaction commit with it, even if the decision then lands as `EXPIRED` (a re-verification record may sit beside an `EXPIRED` transfer). Decided, not yet implemented ([DEC-2026-11-14-02](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0223-owner-delegated-defaults--14-november-2026)): the hook will return an opaque, non-secret `verificationRef` that is stored on the accepted transfer (a CHECK requires it on newly accepted rows; events keep carrying only the transfer id), and an approval without one is a refusal. Implemented ([DEC-2026-11-14-03](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0223-owner-delegated-defaults--14-november-2026)): acceptance re-checks, with the proposing Founder's `User` row read `FOR SHARE` (a suspension is a non-key `UPDATE`, so a concurrent suspension serializes with the acceptance and the later of the two sees the earlier) taken before the Guild row lock, the lock order every command uses (`User`, then Guild; as `activateGuild` does for the draft owner), so a Founder command racing the acceptance cannot deadlock with it, and then, under the Guild and transfer row locks, confirms the proposer is still the Founder, that the proposer is still the Founder and not suspended; otherwise it is refused with `FORBIDDEN`, nothing is applied and the proposal stays `PROPOSED` (the refusal rolls the transaction back, so it leaves no receipt). It is acceptable again if the suspension is lifted inside the window, otherwise it lapses at `expiresAt`. A suspended Founder still cannot act, withdrawal included (`ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED`); acceptance makes withdrawal unnecessary. The recipient can always decline, and no operator gains any power to withdraw, force or apply a transfer. A transfer receipt replayed after the digest-bearing ids became lowercase (an older receipt with uppercase ids) conflicts with `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`; this is pre-production.

Public updates (DEFAULT, not an owner decision): an update is plain text (title ≤200, body ≤4000 characters, trimmed) stored and returned verbatim; consuming UIs must escape it. Its public projection is title, body, publication time and an opaque id: no author, Guild id, state or private configuration (the table has no column for a private roadmap). Drafting is delegated to profile authority, but publishing is the Founder's alone, so approval cannot be delegated; a withdrawn update leaves the public list. An author can withdraw their own draft only while they still hold live profile authority. Withdrawal is allowed while `allowNewWrites` is false (a takedown must not wait for the kill switch); drafting and publishing are not. Rows are never deleted, so drafts withdrawn before publication keep counting against their author (open drafts ≤50 per Guild; open plus withdrawn-unpublished ≤100 per author, then `UPDATE_LIMIT`). The managed view lists drafts and published updates, not withdrawn ones, newest 100 in one query (no cursor yet; an additive keyset cursor whose `before` must name a row the caller may see is decided, not yet implemented, in [DEC-2026-11-14-04](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0223-owner-delegated-defaults--14-november-2026)); a Founder can always find a published update by id in the public list. A public `before` cursor must name an update of this Guild that was published (it may have been withdrawn since the page was served); an unknown, other-Guild or never-published id is `INVALID_INPUT` (HTTP `INVALID_COMMAND`), not an empty last page. Publication time is the database clock at the moment of publication, enforced by the update guard whatever a direct writer supplies. Content and authorship are immutable once written (database guard), publication needs the current Founder and the matching command receipts at commit, and nothing is deleted.

Activation replay (#290): a replayed `activateGuild` returns the Guild as that activation recorded it (revision one), never the later head, and still re-proves the approver's current authority.

Managed view redaction (#290): `readManagedGuild` returns the Founder's account id only to the Founder and to `PROFILE` managers. A caller whose authority is only `WORK`, `TREASURY` and/or `DISPUTE` gets the same strict `ManagedGuild` shape with `founderAccountId: null` (redacted, not unknown); the other fields are the public profile plus the edit `revision`, which identifies nobody. `activateGuild` and `editGuildProfile` results always carry the id, since only an approver, the Founder or a `PROFILE` manager receives them.

Grant view redaction (#318): `revokeGuildRole` (and its replay) returns `grantedByAccountId: null` (redacted, not unknown; the schema field is nullable) to a caller without `PROFILE` authority at that moment, e.g. a `TREASURY`/`DISPUTE`/`WORK` grantee surrendering their grant, because for those kinds the issuer is the Founder. The Founder and profile managers still receive the issuer. Pre-check ordering (#321): an involved caller (Founder, issuer or grantee) revoking an already revoked grant gets `GRANT_REVOKED` before the Guild row lock is taken; strangers still get `FORBIDDEN`. The check is on current authority, not on who issued the grant, so a user who surrenders their own `PROFILE` grant (they hold no `PROFILE` authority afterwards) is redacted too; this is deliberate over-redaction, safe because nothing is revealed that the caller no longer manages. A former Founder can no longer revoke (or replay a revocation, which re-proves current standing and answers `FORBIDDEN` with no data) so the redaction no longer applies to that case.

Profile edit ordering (#310): `editGuildProfile` first judges the caller's standing without a lock (the Founder or any live `PROFILE` grant), so an account with no profile authority gets `FORBIDDEN` for a missing and an existing Guild alike and never queues behind the Guild row; it then takes the Guild row lock and re-judges the exact field scope under it. One database clock reading taken with that row is used both for the policy's `now` and, as a bound parameter, for selecting live grants, so the two can never disagree about a grant created or expiring in between. Redelegation in `grantGuildRole` does the same (#314): the policy check, the per-field delegating-cover bounds that cap the new grant's expiry, and the per-issuer and per-Guild live-grant counts all bind the one instant read with the locked Guild row, and the command fails closed (`FORBIDDEN`) if any requested field shows no covering delegating grant at that instant, so cover that lapses between reads can never yield an unbounded grant. The HTTP edit result is re-parsed with a non-null `founderAccountId`; only a managed read may carry the redacted `null`.

Errors (`GuildStorageError`): `INVALID_INPUT`, `UNAUTHENTICATED`, `ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED`, `FORBIDDEN`, `NOT_FOUND`, `WRITES_DISABLED`, `REVISION_CONFLICT`, `GRANT_REVOKED` (a repeat revocation: the grant is already revoked, so treat it as done (the HTTP transport answers it `GUILD_GRANT_ALREADY_REVOKED`, never `REVISION_CHANGED`); revocation, `WITHDRAW` and `DECLINE` are never blocked by the new-writes switch), `GUILD_EXISTS`, `SLUG_UNAVAILABLE`, `GRANT_LIMIT`, `TRANSFER_PENDING`, `UPDATE_LIMIT`, `STORAGE_CONTRACT`, `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. Permission is judged before the caller's revision, so a stranger learns nothing about it. A deadlock or serialization abort (`40P01`, `40001`) is not a `GuildStorageError`: it is `CommandReceiptError('RETRY_TRANSACTION')`, to be retried as a whole transaction.

## Public HTTP reads

`createGuildPublicHandlers(host)` (`src/guild-profiles/guild-public-http.ts`) is a framework-free Request/Response factory for unauthenticated reads (the two below, plus the directory described after them): `GET /api/v1/guilds/{slug}` (the approved public projection: `id`, `slug`, `name`, `description`, `primaryToken`) and `GET /api/v1/guilds/{slug}/updates?limit&before` (published updates as `{id, title, body, publishedAt}`, page ≤20, `nextCursor`). The host supplies `version:'v1'`, `requestId`, `available` (the feature flag), `rateLimit`, `guild` and `updates`; it owns SQL, the transaction and the throttle. Order of checks: method (405), flag (a closed flag answers `NOT_FOUND`, touching nothing), slug and strict query (unknown or repeated parameters, malformed `limit`/`before` → `INVALID_COMMAND`), throttle (429), read. Every host result is re-parsed with a strict schema and the profile's slug must equal the requested slug; a malformed, over-shared or wrong-Guild result becomes `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`, never a forwarded body. Responses are `Cache-Control: no-store` so a withdrawn update disappears at once; failures use fixed, sanitized envelopes. The Root host (`src/lib/guild-public-host.ts`, `guild-public-http.ts`, routes under `src/app/api/v1/guilds/[slug]`) reads in one command transaction, throttles at 120 requests per 60 seconds per client and is gated by `GUILD_PUBLIC_READS_ENABLED` (exact `true`, default off). No write, session or private field is added by these two reads; the public page below renders the same reads. A closed Guild's profile is still exposed once active; there is no unlisting yet (OPEN). **Directory (T015):** `listPublicGuilds(tx, {limit?, after?})` returns active Guilds in slug order as `{guilds:[{slug,name,description}], nextCursor}` (page ≤20, default 20); `after` is the last slug already seen (keyset on the unique `Guild_slug_key` index, so no migration), `nextCursor` is the last slug of a full page and null at the end, and a slug that names no Guild just continues after it. Entries carry no Guild id, Founder, token or state; each row passes the same `projectPublicGuildProfile` approval as `readPublicGuild`, then a strict directory schema, so a malformed row is `STORAGE_CONTRACT`, never forwarded. `GET /api/v1/guilds?limit&after` is `handlers.GUILDS(request)` with the same method/flag/throttle/error mapping and `no-store` headers as the other reads; unknown or repeated parameters, a bad `limit` or a malformed slug cursor are `INVALID_COMMAND`. The host member `guilds(query)` is optional (adding it is backward compatible): a host that omits it answers that route as not found; when present it must be a function. The Root host wires `readGuildDirectoryFromHost` (`src/lib/guild-public-host.ts`) behind the same `GUILD_PUBLIC_READS_ENABLED` flag and `guild-public` budget.

## Public Guild page

`/guilds/{slug}` (`src/app/guilds/[slug]/page.tsx`, with `loading.tsx` and `not-found.tsx`) is the server-rendered visitor view of the same projection (T015 slice, R08/R10, I06/I07, AC42). Its data layer `src/lib/guild-public-page.ts` calls the same host reads as the public API (`readGuildFromHost` → `readPublicGuild`, `readGuildUpdatesFromHost` → `listPublicGuildUpdates`), behind the same `GUILD_PUBLIC_READS_ENABLED` flag, and re-parses both results with the same strict schemas (slug must match). It renders only the name, slug, description, primary-token mint (when set) and published updates (title, body as escaped plain text, `publishedAt`), ten per page; the internal Guild id, Founder, authors, drafts and withdrawn updates never reach HTML, metadata or the hydration payload (the page is a server component and passes no data to client code). "Load more updates" is an ordinary link to `?before=<cursor>` (the public cursor) and "Back to latest updates" returns to the first page; other query parameters are ignored. Flag off, a malformed slug or cursor, or an unknown Guild all render the same not-found page (because the root `loading.tsx` streams, it is a soft not-found: HTTP 200 with `noindex`, as on other dynamic pages); other read failures reach the error boundary. `generateMetadata` uses only the name and description (generic "Guild not found" otherwise) and sets `noindex` while unlisting is OPEN. The route is `force-dynamic` with no data cache, so a withdrawn update is gone on the next request. Like the API, the page is throttled at 120 requests per 60 seconds per client (same `publicRateLimit` scope `guild-public`, same `TRUSTED_IP_HEADER` client key and shared-bucket fallback, so page and API views share one budget). The throttle runs after the flag and slug checks and before any storage read, once per request (metadata and page share it); over the limit the page renders a "Too many requests" notice and generic `noindex` metadata, reads nothing and cannot set a 429 status because the response is already streaming (see the soft-404 note: Next.js fixes the status when `loading.tsx` first streams). A non-429 limiter failure reaches the error boundary. ## Management HTTP for public updates

`createGuildUpdatesHandlers(host)` (`src/guild-profiles/guild-updates-http.ts`) is the authenticated Request/Response transport for the update commands: `GET /api/v1/guild-updates?guildId=` (the caller's managed view plus `writesAvailable`), `POST /api/v1/guild-updates` (`{guildId,title,body}` → draft) and `POST /api/v1/guild-updates/{updateId}` (`{guildId,action:'PUBLISH'|'WITHDRAW'}`). The host supplies `actor` (its own cookie-derived session; an actor, account or role in the body is a strict-schema failure), `checkOrigin`, `rateLimit`, `list`, `draft`, `publish` and `withdraw`. POSTs check origin, then the actor, then the throttle, then the `Idempotency-Key` header and a bounded strict JSON body; GET authenticates and throttles but needs no origin. Authorization stays in storage (Founder or live `PROFILE` grantee drafts, Founder alone publishes, author-or-Founder withdraws); the transport only maps results: every host result is re-parsed strictly and bound to the request (update id, Guild id, the resulting state for a fresh result, and the drafted title/text), a mismatch becomes `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. Responses are `private, no-store` with `Vary: Cookie`; failures use fixed envelopes (`FORBIDDEN`, `NOT_FOUND`, `REVISION_CHANGED` for an update already moved, the new `GUILD_UPDATE_LIMIT_REACHED`, `NOT_AVAILABLE` for disabled writes, `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, `RATE_LIMITED`). The Root host (`src/lib/guild-updates-host.ts`, `guild-updates-http.ts`, routes under `src/app/api/v1/guild-updates`) throttles commands at 30 per 60 seconds and reads at 120 per 60 seconds per account, in separate buckets so polling cannot block a publication or takedown. The Root host runs each command through `runCommandTransaction`, which already retries the whole transaction on deadlock or serialization failure (bounded by attempts and 15 seconds) before surfacing `RETRY_TRANSACTION`; that code and `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` map to a retryable `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` (503), so no further retry belongs in the transport (#300). `writesAvailable` in a read and the `allowNewWrites` used by the next command are read from the environment at different instants; if the flag flips between them a UI may show writes enabled and receive `NOT_AVAILABLE`, which is harmless. An idempotent replay may find the update already moved on and still succeeds, and the state a fresh result must show is checked only for `EXECUTED`: a replayed draft may be in any state (`DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN`; title, text and Guild id must still match), a replayed publish may show `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and a replayed withdrawal must show `WITHDRAWN`; a fresh draft must be `DRAFT`, a fresh publish `PUBLISHED` and a fresh withdrawal `WITHDRAWN`. `status` is supplied by the host and trusted by the transport: a host that labels a fresh result `REPLAYED` skips only that exact-state check, never the Guild, update id or content binding, so the host must set `REPLAYED` only for a result it took from a stored receipt. `GUILD_UPDATES_ENABLED` (exact `true`, default off) gates only new drafts and publications; own reads and withdrawal stay available so a takedown never waits for the flag. A Founder exists in production only after the operator activation route below has approved a draft; no UI is added.

Publish and withdraw judge the caller's standing before taking any lock (publish needs Founder status; withdraw needs the Founder or a live `PROFILE` authority), so an account with no authority in a Guild cannot queue behind its row and gets the same `FORBIDDEN` for a missing Guild, a missing update and an existing update; standing is rechecked under the lock. A replay of a publish or withdraw (same idempotency key) re-checks that live authority too, in addition to identity (implemented, [DEC-2026-11-14-05](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0223-owner-delegated-defaults--14-november-2026) b, from DEC-2026-11-13-05 b): a publish replay needs the account to still be the Founder, a withdraw replay Founder status or a live `PROFILE` grant, and an account that has since lost it gets `FORBIDDEN` and no update data. Requests are throttled before the body is validated, as at the draft and work boundaries, so malformed requests spend budget. `runCommandTransaction` retries whole transactions on deadlock and serialization failures; only exhaustion (`RETRY_TRANSACTION`) or an unmapped storage failure reaches the client, as a generic retryable `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. `writesAvailable` in a read and the `allowNewWrites` of the next command are read from the environment at different instants, so a flag flipped in between can show writes enabled and then answer `NOT_AVAILABLE`.

## Role grant and ownership transfer HTTP

`createGuildRolesHandlers(host)` (`src/guild-profiles/guild-roles-http.ts`) is the authenticated Request/Response transport for `grantGuildRole`, `revokeGuildRole`, `proposeOwnershipTransfer` and `respondOwnershipTransfer` (T009, #51). Routes: `POST /api/v1/guild-grants` (the grant input of the storage command), `POST /api/v1/guild-grants/{grantId}/revoke` (`{guildId}`), `POST /api/v1/guild-ownership-transfers` (`{guildId,toAccountId,expiresAt}`) and `POST /api/v1/guild-ownership-transfers/{transferId}` (`{guildId,decision:'ACCEPT'|'DECLINE'|'WITHDRAW'}`), plus `GET /api/v1/guild-ownership-transfers`, the one read (see below). Routes are flat and take the Guild id in the body like the other management routes (the `/guilds/{slug}` namespace stays public read-only). Every command call checks origin, then the cookie-derived actor, then the per-account command throttle (30 per 60 seconds, bucket `guild-roles`), then the `Idempotency-Key` header and a bounded strict JSON body. An actor, issuer, session or role in the body is a strict-schema failure. Authorization is storage's alone: the Founder or a delegating `PROFILE` grantee grants within its own fields and expiry, the Founder, issuer or grantee revokes, the Founder proposes, the recipient accepts or declines and the proposing Founder withdraws; a stranger and a caller from another Guild get `FORBIDDEN`, and a grant or transfer id that is not in the named Guild is not found (nothing about other Guilds is revealed).

Pending transfer read (#294 NOTE): `GET /api/v1/guild-ownership-transfers` takes no query (any parameter is `INVALID_COMMAND`) and answers `{version:'v1', transfers:[…], truncated}` for the caller's own session only. It checks the session actor and then the read throttle (120 per 60 seconds, bucket `guild-roles-read`); it needs no origin check and no `Idempotency-Key`, and it is not gated by `GUILD_ROLE_MANAGEMENT_ENABLED`, so a recipient can always see what is asked of them. Only the two parties of an open (`PROPOSED`, not past `expiresAt`) proposal see it: the recipient and the proposing Founder. Another Guild member, a delegate or a stranger gets an empty list and cannot tell whether a proposal exists. The proposer's account id goes only to the proposer: the recipient's row has `fromAccountId: null` (redacted, not unknown), as `ManagedGuild.founderAccountId` is for callers without profile authority, so an unsolicited proposal discloses no account; the recipient still gets the Guild id and transfer id needed to respond. At most 50 proposals come back, soonest expiry first, and `truncated: true` says more exist. The transport re-checks every row against the caller (an open proposal, the caller as proposer or as recipient with a null proposer); anything else, or a missing `truncated`, is `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. The same redaction applies to the `RESPOND` result (#448): the recipient's ACCEPT or DECLINE result, fresh or replayed, has `fromAccountId: null`; the proposing Founder's WITHDRAW and PROPOSE results carry their own id. The transport refuses a recipient result that names the proposer (`SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`).

Every host result is re-parsed strictly and bound to the request: the grant must be for the requested Guild, grantee, kind, fields, delegation right and expiry, issued by the caller, and live when fresh; a revocation must show the requested grant revoked, fresh or replayed; a proposal must come from the caller to the requested recipient and expiry and be open when fresh; a response must be the requested decision, or the lapse that overtook it (`EXPIRED`, `applied: false`, still `status: 'EXECUTED'`: clients must read `transfer.state`), with `applied` true only for `ACCEPTED`. A mismatch is `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. A replay never fails because the world moved on (a replayed grant may show `revokedAt`, a replayed proposal may show a decided state). The validated grant body is forwarded as sent because storage parses and normalizes it itself. `grantedByAccountId` is exactly what storage returns: the issuer for the Founder and profile managers, `null` for a surrendering `WORK`/`TREASURY`/`DISPUTE` grantee. Responses are `private, no-store` with `Vary: Cookie`; failures use fixed envelopes: `FORBIDDEN`, `NOT_FOUND`, `REVISION_CHANGED` (a changed profile or an already decided transfer), `GUILD_GRANT_ALREADY_REVOKED` (409, not retryable: a repeated revocation with a fresh key, #292-4; the grant is already as the caller wanted, so treat it as done and do not re-read and retry; the same key still replays `200`), the new `GUILD_GRANT_LIMIT_REACHED` and `GUILD_TRANSFER_PENDING`, `NOT_AVAILABLE`, `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `INVALID_COMMAND`.

The Root host (`src/lib/guild-roles-host.ts`, `guild-roles-http.ts`, `guild-http-session.ts`, routes under `src/app/api/v1/guild-grants` and `guild-ownership-transfers`) gates new grants, proposals and acceptances on `GUILD_ROLE_MANAGEMENT_ENABLED` (exact `true`, default off, never in demo mode); revocation, withdrawal and decline are never gated, so taking authority away stays possible in an incident. The Root ownership policy hook (`authorizeOwnershipTransfer`) answers **closed**: the recorded project-control re-verification it stands for (O11/G10, T010) does not exist, so no acceptance moves a Founder over HTTP in production; declining and withdrawing do not need it. A Founder exists in production only after the activation route (#305, below) has approved a draft, and no UI is added (the recipient of a proposal finds it through the `GET` list). Current behavior: a former Founder stays `grantedBy` on what it issued while Founder but cannot revoke it after a transfer, `TREASURY`/`DISPUTE` included (issuer revoke power follows current authority, [DEC-2026-11-13-03](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0222-owner-delegated-decisions--13-november-2026) a); `WITHDRAW` and `DECLINE` are exempt from the flag and the kill switch like revocation, while `PROPOSE` and `ACCEPT` stay gated (b). Replays of publish/withdraw will re-check live authority ([DEC-2026-11-13-05](../RAID_AGENT_BLUEPRINT.md#0222-owner-delegated-decisions--13-november-2026)).

## Activation HTTP (#305, DEC-2026-11-07-02)

`createGuildActivationHandlers(host)` (`src/guild-profiles/guild-activation-http.ts`) is the authenticated Request/Response transport for `activateGuild`: `POST /api/v1/guild-activations` takes the storage input `{guildDraftId,expectedDraftRevision,activationRef}` (strict; an actor, approver, Founder or session in the body is a 422) and answers `{status:'EXECUTED'|'REPLAYED', guild}` where `guild` is the revision-one `ManagedGuild` including the new Founder's account id (an approver always receives it). The host supplies `version:'v1'`, `requestId`, `actor` (its own cookie-derived session), `checkOrigin`, `rateLimit` and `activate`. Order: origin, then actor, then throttle, then the `Idempotency-Key` header and a bounded JSON body. The transport re-parses every host result strictly and refuses (as `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`, never a forwarded body) one that is malformed, not revision one, or names the caller as Founder. Responses are `private, no-store` with `Vary: Cookie`; `GUILD_EXISTS` and `SLUG_UNAVAILABLE` stay opaque `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. `activationRef` is a free-form label (`^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._:/-]*$`, up to 200 characters) naming whatever evidence the operator relied on; until T010 defines the evidence record the platform does not parse it and nothing treats it as verification.

The Root host (`src/lib/guild-activation-host.ts`, `guild-activation-http.ts`, `guild-http-session.ts`, route `src/app/api/v1/guild-activations/route.ts`) throttles at 30 requests per 60 seconds per account in its own `guild-activation` bucket and decides approval in `authorizeActivation`:

- **Switch:** `GUILD_ACTIVATION_ENABLED` (exact `true`, default off, never in demo mode) gates only new activations. An original receipt still replays with the switch off, and a replay re-proves the approver.
- **Allowlist:** `GUILD_ACTIVATION_OPERATOR_ACCOUNT_IDS`, a comma-separated list of account ids, exact match, trimmed, blank entries ignored, read on every call so removing an account takes effect at once. Unset or empty denies everyone. It is a separate list from `RAID_WORK_OPERATOR_ACCOUNT_IDS` (Raid-listing authority): a work operator is not an activation operator and no other list or role is consulted. With the switch on and the list empty, startup configuration validation warns.
- **No self-approval:** `activateGuild` itself refuses an approver who owns the draft, whatever the host answers. The real-database test (`tests/guild-activation-http-db.test.ts`) proves it for an applicant who is also on the allowlist, with the switch on.
- **Audit:** the existing private `guild.activated.v1` audit event (actor = the approver, no outbox) is written under the same command receipt as the Guild, its first profile version and the stored approver (`Guild.activatedByAccountId`) and `activationRef`; the follow-up guard migration rejects a version without that event at commit. A refused or failed activation leaves no Guild, version, receipt or event.
- **Simulated funds only:** an activation records no reward, escrow or balance and moves no funds, and it fails closed while any of `LAUNCH_ENABLED`, `PAYOUT_ENABLED`, `X_TRACKING_V2_SETTLEMENT_ENABLED` or `LIVE_COLLECTION_APPROVED` is exactly `true` (the truthiness those flags use): new activations are unavailable and the approver is denied, replays included, until the switch is off again.
- **Not the trust root:** any allowlisted operator can create a Founder from any draft, so this is NOT the T010 project-control evidence (O11/G10). It keeps Guild creation separate from Raid-listing authority and nothing more. The Founder, token holders and the applicant gain no authority from it, and no UI, doc or response should be read as saying project control has been verified. T010 stays open; admin approval does not replace control evidence for existing projects (blueprint 05.5).

The operator learns a draft's id and revision from the applicant, since no read endpoint or queue exists for operators yet (not added here).

Not yet available: a UI or operator queue for activation, a read endpoint for pending drafts, a UI for role grants, revocation and ownership transfer, and any T010 evidence record.

## Host dependencies and transaction rules

Inject the existing `CommandReceiptTransaction` (PostgreSQL, READ COMMITTED) and an explicit `allowNewWrites` boolean; for activation also `authorizeActivation`. The host owns cookie authentication (the `GuildActor` is the account and the SHA-256 of the current session cookie), origin/rate/body limits for any future transport, whole-transaction retry and commit-before-response. Receipt-first ordering: the command receipt, then the actor's `User` (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`), `Session` (`FOR SHARE`); activation then takes the draft owner's `User` row (`FOR SHARE`) before the draft row (`FOR UPDATE`), the same User-before-draft order as the draft writer, so a draft save and an activation by another approver cannot deadlock (two approvers who each own a draft the other activates still can; Postgres aborts one and the host's whole-transaction retry covers it); an edit takes the Guild row (`FOR UPDATE`). Authority is re-proven on the database clock after each row wait and on replay; an expired or revoked session, a suspended account or a revoked grant rolls the whole command back. A replayed edit re-proves that the actor's current authority covers the same fields and returns the Guild as that stored profile version recorded it, never later revisions; a replayed activation re-proves the approver.

## Storage, audit and recovery

Migrations `202610270008_guilds`, `202610270009_guild_ownership_transfer`, `202610270010_guild_updates`, `202610290001_guild_fk_indexes`, `202611050001_guild_followup_guards`, `202611130001_guild_ownership_followups`, `202611130002_guild_ownership_lapse_successor_time`, `202611130007_guild_role_separation` and `202611130008_guild_role_separation_recheck` (`202610290001_guild_fk_indexes` adds indexes on `Guild.activatedByAccountId`, `GuildProfileVersion.actorAccountId` and `GuildRoleGrant.grantedByAccountId`) add `Guild` (stable unique slug, fixed Founder, activation record, one Guild per source draft), `GuildProfileVersion` (immutable, each tied to its command receipt) and `GuildRoleGrant`. Guards reject a changed id, slug, Founder, activation record or source draft; a revision without its own new version; deleting a Guild, version or grant; and any grant edit except one-time revocation. Every activation and edit records a private audit event (identifier-only payload `guildProfileVersionId`, no outbox). The shared event envelope gains the additive keys `guildProfileVersionId`, `guildRoleGrantId`, `guildOwnershipTransferId` and `guildUpdateId`. `202610290001_guild_fk_indexes` uses plain `CREATE INDEX` inside the migration transaction, which takes a `SHARE` lock (blocking writes) on `Guild`, `GuildProfileVersion` and `GuildRoleGrant` while it builds: harmless on new small tables, but a large existing environment should schedule it in a quiet window or build the indexes `CONCURRENTLY` outside a transaction first. Backup/restore must keep Guilds, versions, grants, receipts and audit together; the migration is additive and has no destructive down step once used. `202611050001_guild_followup_guards` adds three guards and changes no table or data (#290, #296): a deferred trigger on `GuildProfileVersion` that, at commit, requires the actor's `ActivateGuild` receipt (revision one) or `EditGuildProfile` receipt (later revisions), its first private audit event naming the version with no outbox row, and a Guild projection that points at this version or has moved past it, so a privileged direct writer can no longer leave a stray version that blocks later edits; a `GuildRoleGrant_revoked_check` CHECK that a grant is revoked at or after its `validFrom` (`revokeGuildRole` records `GREATEST(now, validFrom)`, so a future-dated row written by a non-command writer stays revocable); and a replacement of the update guard function that stamps `publishedAt` with the database clock on publication, whatever a direct writer supplies, so the public feed and its cursor cannot be misordered. A privileged writer who can also fabricate receipts and audit rows remains outside these guards, as before. `202611130001_guild_ownership_followups` (example: `followup-ownership.sql`) changes no table or data (#294 item 11): the decision CHECK states its identical decided-state clause once, and the commit-time transfer guard now accepts a lapse recorded by the Founder's next `ProposeOwnershipTransfer` only when that receipt is the proposing Founder's own and created a successor transfer in the same Guild (a lapse has no response command, so its `decideReceiptId` is the observing proposal's receipt, whose `resultRef` is the successor, not the lapsed row; the expiry instant is `expiresAt`). Existing rows are not re-validated. `202611130002_guild_ownership_lapse_successor_time` (example: `followup-lapse-successor.sql`; #438 item 1) replaces that guard again, changing no table or data, and adds that the successor was proposed at or after the lapsed transfer's `expiresAt`, so a lapse cannot be attributed to an older proposal of the same Founder in the same Guild; `202611130001` itself is not edited. A response that finds its transfer already decided inside its own transaction answers `REVISION_CONFLICT` rather than `STORAGE_CONTRACT`. The draft owner's `User` row stays `FOR SHARE` at activation, not `FOR KEY SHARE`: a suspension is a non-key update, which `KEY SHARE` does not block, so it could commit between the suspended check and the new Guild (#290 item 7). That share lock is held to commit, so it blocks every other update of that owner's `User` row for the length of the activation, including one made by a host `authorizeActivation` hook, which must not write that row. Two approvers who each own the draft the other activates can therefore deadlock: Postgres aborts one with `40P01`, and the module reports it as `CommandReceiptError('RETRY_TRANSACTION')` (also `40001`, from either the SQLSTATE or Prisma's raw-query `meta.code`), never `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. The host retries the whole transaction (the Root host's `runCommandTransaction` and the example host already do; a host port may also classify it itself) (#438 item 2).

The independent host also drives the role and ownership handlers (a stranger refused, a grant, a surrender, a proposal and a decline over HTTP) and the public HTTP handlers with real `Request`/`Response` objects.
